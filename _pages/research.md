I am interested in exploring the physical properties of quantum materials and nonequilibrium order parameter dynamics, with a special focus on nontrivial topology in real space as well as in the band structure, and using topological concepts as an organizing principle. The intersection of these themes with quantum information opens up new possibilities for taking advantage of topological phenomena and nonequilibrium dynamics when designing quantum functionalities. In particular, I am interested in engineering magnetic environments to enable entanglement between qubits and leveraging topological spin textures as building blocks for quantum computation.

My Ph.D. research investigated topological charge transport across a broad range of physical systems, leveraging phenomenology and computational methods. My works were guided by the central questions:

- (i) How do we design systems that are natural hosts for topological charges?
- (ii) What tools are available to manipulate topological charge transport?

Working within a hydrodynamic framework, I systematically developed field-theoretic formalism and performed numerical studies to probe topological structures, looking for functionalities that may potentially enable next-generation technologies. Building on the foundations developed in my Ph.D., I aim to explore three complementary directions in my future work: leveraging quantum topological spin textures for sensing and quantum computing technologies, quantum reservoir engineering, and exotic quantum material.
